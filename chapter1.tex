\chapter{Real numbers, complex numbers, quaternions, and vectors.}

\section{Number lines, planes, spaces, and hyperspaces.}

\textbullet \; The real number line. \\

Consider all of the real numbers. They can be thought of as living or residing on the real number line, which we denote
as \(\mathbb{R}\). \\

\textbullet \; The real number plane, aka the 2-dimensional real number plane. \\

Now consider all of the numbers which can reside within a 2-dimensional real number plane. 

Cartesian co-ordinate
system, i.e. the familiar \(x\)-\(y\) co-ordinates. They can be thought of as living or residing in a 2-dimensional real
number space, which we denote as \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Next consider the complex numbers. They also exist within a
2-dimensional co-ordinate system which is often represented using an Argand diagram. But how do we denote this
2-dimensional complex number space? We can use \(\mathbb{R}^2\) in this case as well, but it won't tell the full story.
because one of the axes that comprise the space is
imaginary and for that reason, the relationship between the real and imaginary axes is different than for
\(\mathbb{R}^2\). \\


\section{Denoting vectors and quaternions.}

In this article, we will denote quaternions using uppercase letters, while vectors will be denoted using either lowercase
or uppercase letters which have been adorned with a right pointing arrow above them. Examples of how vectors will be denoted are
shown immediately below;

\begin{equation*}
\vec{v} \quad \vec{V} \quad \vec{q} \quad \vec{Q}
\end{equation*}
